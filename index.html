Ψ-Continuum

From Cosmic Expansion Diagnostics
to Effective Gravitational Sources

What can cosmic expansion reveal about gravity?

Ψ-Continuum investigates whether the observed late-time expansion of the Universe differs from the standard ΛCDM model, and what effective gravitational sources could correspond to such differences under explicitly stated assumptions of general relativity.

01 / Research Question

A reference model.
A question for the data.

Observations support an accelerated late-time expansion of the Universe. The standard ΛCDM model describes this history using a cosmological constant, cold dark matter and ordinary matter within general relativity.

Testing possible departures asks how well that description holds across datasets. A deviation alone does not establish new physics: reference parameters, measurement uncertainty and systematic effects must be checked before interpretation. Follow the method →

02 / Methodology

From Data to Physics

Step 1

Observe

Constrain the expansion history H(z) using Type Ia supernovae (SN Ia), baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) and expansion-rate measurements, with their uncertainties.

Step 2

Diagnose

Represent departures from a specified reference with the macroscopic state-space coordinate Ψ(z). The result depends on the reference model and its parameters.

Step 3

Reconstruct

Use Einstein’s equations within an explicitly specified FLRW cosmology to infer compatible effective energy density and pressure. State curvature, matter content and reconstruction assumptions.

Reproducibility & model consistency · September 2026

Results & Scientific Audit

A systematic reproducibility and model-consistency audit of the frozen Psi-Continuum v2 numerical implementation has been completed.

What was reproduced

The audit confirms that the archived numerical pipeline is internally reproducible. Non-zero-amplitude preferences belong to the implemented frozen model, not the documented v2 equation.

The original v2 repository remains frozen and unchanged to preserve the scientific record. The separate audit provides the full numerical trace, validation scripts, and results.

What remains unresolved

The model–implementation mismatch is established: for non-zero response amplitude, the frozen implementation does not correspond to the defining equation documented for v2.

A scientifically interpretable inference for the documented equation requires a separately specified analysis with consistent supernova calibration and BAO conventions. Reproducibility of the frozen pipeline does not resolve this inference.

Future Ψ-Continuum studies require independently specified, data-validated and fully traceable computational investigations.

This audit concerns the frozen v2 implementation; it does not validate every stage of the research program or establish new physics.

Experimental Program & KTRO Observatory

Observational layer · measurement stability · reproducible monitoring

Alongside theoretical and computational work, the Psi-Continuum framework also includes an emerging observational program centered on measurement stability, long-term reproducibility, and response-based diagnostics in real data.

A central part of this direction is KTRO — Klimov Tarpen Robotic Observatory, a compact observational initiative designed to support differential photometry, monitoring consistency, and small-scale experimental validation of response-oriented methods.

This observational layer connects the conceptual framework to real measurement practice and extends the project beyond theory and computation alone.
